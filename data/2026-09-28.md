### [1] [A new type of skin thickness in high-spin isomers to constrain equation of state of spin-polarized nuclear matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30710)
*Toi Tachibana,Kouichi Hagino,Kenichi Yoshida,Qiang Zhao*

Main category: nucl-th

TL;DR: 本文研究自旋极化核物质状态方程（EOS）的实验探针，利用相对论点耦合模型计算 ^{52}Fe 核的 12^+ 同核异能态，发现高自旋态的自旋皮层厚度与 EOS 的自旋斜率参数强关联，并指出均方电荷半径可作为有效的实验探针。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 为约束自旋极化核物质的状态方程（EOS）寻找可靠的实验探针，类比中子皮层厚度与对称能斜率参数之间的线性关联，探索自旋自由度的对应可观测量。

Method: 采用相对论点耦合模型（relativistic point-coupling model）计算 ^{52}Fe 核的 12^+ 同核异能态；定义自旋皮层厚度为自旋向上密度半径与自旋向下密度半径之差，并研究其与 EOS 自旋斜率参数的关联；同时考察 12^+ 态与基态半径之差以及均方电荷半径。

Result: 自旋皮层厚度与 EOS 自旋斜率参数存在强线性关联；即便改用 12^+ 态与基态半径之差，关联依然保持，但显著减弱；均方电荷半径平方后对自旋斜率参数的依赖显著增强，可作为良好的实验探针。

Conclusion: 自旋皮层厚度（尤其是自旋向上与自旋向下半径之差）是约束自旋极化核物质 EOS 的有力探针，均方电荷半径在高分辨率激光谱学实验条件下亦可有效表征自旋斜率参数。

Abstract: We explore experimental probes for constraining the equation of state (EOS) of spin-polarized nuclear matter, where spins of nucleons are aligned along a particular direction. For this purpose, we calculate the $12^+$ isomeric state of the \ce{^{52}Fe} nucleus with the relativistic point-coupling model. We argue that the spin skin thickness of this high-spin state, that is, the difference between the radius of the spin-up density and that of the spin-down density, strongly correlates with the spin slope parameter of the EOS. This is in analogy to the well known linear correlation between the slope parameter of the symmetry energy and the neutron skin thickness of finite nuclei. We show that this correlation is retained even when one considers the difference of the radii between the $12^+$ and the ground states, even though the correlation is much weaker than in the case of the difference between the spin-up and the spin-down radii. We also discuss the mean-square charge radii of the $12^+$ state. We find that by taking the square of the radii the dependence on the spin slope parameter becomes much stronger, and thus it can serve as a good probe of the spin slope parameter given a high resolution of laser-spectroscopy experiments.
